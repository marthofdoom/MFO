## 2. The established pattern

1. **Two tiers is the norm. Three or four is the exception.** 11 of 17 games use 2 tiers. Gaiden, Echoes, Radiant Dawn and Sacred Stones (for trainees) use 3. Only Three Houses uses 4. No game uses 5.
2. **The gate is level plus one more key.** Every game gates by level (10, 20, 30 are the standard numbers). The second key changed over time: a class item (FE1 to FE7), a universal seal (FE8 onward), skill ranks via an exam (Three Houses), weapon proficiency (Engage). Three Houses and Engage gate on DEMONSTRATED COMPETENCE in the disciplines the new class uses. No game gates on "mastered N classes of the previous tier". Three Houses does not require you to have held, let alone mastered, any earlier class [V5].
3. **Each promotion is a 2-way choice.** From Sacred Stones on, a generic class promotes into one of 2 classes (Sacred Stones, Awakening, Fates, Engage). Trainees get 3. Three Houses is the outlier: a wide menu filtered by your ranks.
4. **One branch stays pure, the other crosses lines.** The 2-way choice usually pairs a deeper version of the same line with a cross-line hybrid [M for the exact pairs]. So every pure build always has a path to the top tier. In Three Houses the TOP tier is where hybrids live, but pure units still reach it because the secondary requirement is low (Lance C next to Bow A).
5. **The top tier carries a signature power.** Radiant Dawn grants a mastery skill on the third promotion [V4]. Three Houses keeps Advanced and Master combat arts locked to their class [V2]. Engage class skills only work in that class [V4]. The top tier's best tool stays with the class.
6. **What you learn travels, within a slot limit.** Awakening and Fates keep every learned skill but equip 5 [V4]. Three Houses equips 5 portable abilities next to 3 class abilities [V4]. Engage equips 2 inherited skills [V4]. Learned power is permanent, but its breadth is capped by slots.
7. **Two layers: the active class bonus and the earned portable bonus.** Three Houses is the clearest case: class abilities (active only while in the class) plus mastery abilities (portable once earned) [V2][V4]. This is the same shape as marth's "equipped class gives a stronger rank, mastered class gives a permanent passive".
8. **Free switching among unlocked classes won.** The series went from no reclass (FE1 to FE10), to free reclass in the DS remakes [V3], to a seal that reset level (Awakening), to a seal that kept level (Fates), to free swapping among certified classes (Three Houses) [V3].
9. **The series closed grind loops.** The wiki notes that the DS remakes kept level on reclass to stop repeated level grinding, that Awakening's level reset enabled heavy grinding of stats and skills, and that Fates kept level, kept the tier and limited sets to stop that abuse [V3]. Engage allows a reset only at level 20 [V3].
10. **XP thresholds rise with tier, about 1 : 1.7 : 2.5 : 3.3.** Three Houses class XP: 60, 100, 150, 200 battles [V2].
11. **Mastery XP rewards participation, not kills.** Three Houses gives 1 class XP per battle even to a unit that did not attack, and also credits white magic and gambits [V2][V4]. Healers never fall behind.
12. **A trainee start.** Sacred Stones trainees (Journeyman, Recruit, Pupil) and the Echoes Villager start broad and weak and pick a line early [V6][V7]. That is MFO's tier 1 (Melee, Ranged, Mage).

## 4. Is the Fire Emblem pattern better?

Not as a whole. MFO's design is already the Three Houses model (portable mastery passives, in-class bonus, free switching, multi-discipline top tier), and Three Houses is the series' most developed take on it. The older 2-way promotion tree is simpler but suits a linear campaign with permanent choices. MFO's free switching makes that a weaker fit.

FE is better on four points, and these are the recommended changes:

- **R1. The breadth gate should be light, not a second full commitment.** Three Houses asks for one discipline at A plus a secondary at C. MFO's "master 2 of the previous tier" makes the second class cost as much as the first. For an AI follower this hurts more than in FE: to unlock the Knight line, a follower has to fight 3 h as a Mage or archer with that style's gambits. Keep marth's rule ("at least 2 mastered"), but let tier 1 count fully (all three tier-1 classes are 3 h) and, for the pure tier-3 promotions, let the second mastery be any class of the previous tier (eval B's form). If marth wants a stricter FE-style gate later, a "primary mastered, secondary half-mastered" rule is the Three Houses equivalent. That is a ruling for marth.
- **R2. XP by participation.** Fights won while in the party are the main source. Kills are a bonus. Heals, wards, summons and buffs count. This is Three Houses' rule and it keeps support classes on pace.
- **R3. Bound the portable passives.** Either make permanent ranks small (for example the permanent rank about a third of the equipped rank, and no two permanents from the same trait stack), or give the follower 5 passive slots filled automatically by best fit with a manual override. Slots are the FE answer, but they add UI work. Small ranks with no slots is simpler and fits AI followers that never touch a menu.
- **R4. Top tiers keep a class-locked signature.** Tier-3 and Master classes should each give one signature perk or gambit that works only while equipped (Radiant Dawn mastery skill, Three Houses Advanced and Master combat arts). This keeps the active class meaningful once a follower has many masteries.
- **R5. Optional auto-pick on unlock.** Path of Radiance and Radiant Dawn auto-promote. An "auto class" toggle matches MFO's rule that every auto-behavior has a manual override.

Also keep these as they stand: tier 1 as three trainee-style classes, the trait-pair hybrid rule for tier 3, the pure tier-3 line, free switching, and the 3 / 6 / 10 h curve.

## 5. Notes for a Skyrim follower specifically
- In FE the player moves every unit. In MFO the AI fights in whatever style the class gives. So a forced detour class is real play time spent in a style the player did not want. That is why R1 matters more here than in Three Houses.
- Free switching means the active class is a choice the player can change in a menu, but most followers will sit in one class for hours. Equipped-only signatures (R4) make that choice feel like a real build decision.
- FE's anti-grind lesson (do not reset levels on reclass) does not apply here, because MFO has no class level reset. The open grind loop is kill-farming for class XP, which R2 also closes.

## 6. Merging with Skyrim builds: freedom without bloat, with a strong class feel

marth's added criterion: "We'll need to merge the findings with Skyrim builds which are much more diverse. We need freedom without bloat, but still with a strong classes feel."

Skyrim builds mix any armor, any weapon style and any schools, with perks chosen freely. FE classes are much tighter: a class fixes the weapon types and often the movement type. So the lesson is to take FE's STRUCTURE (tiers, mastery, signature powers) but not its LOCKS.

### The three axes, applied to each proposal

| Proposal | Freedom (does an odd build find a class?) | No bloat | Strong class feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round-1 rule (novel pairs only) | Poor. Pure builds stop at tier 2 | 10 tier 3 | Good for the hybrids that exist |
| Eval A round 2 (7 pure + 11 hybrid) | Good for the common builds. Odd armor mixes (Cloth + 2H, Heavy + Illusion) depend on merges | 18 tier 3 | Good, but Templar vs Crusader and Sword-Singer vs Champion are near duplicates |
| Eval B round 2 (9 pure + 10 hybrid) | Good. Every tier-2 class has a pure successor | 19 tier 3 | Champion vs Barbarian differ only by armor |
| All 25 pairs as classes | Full | 25 + 9 pure = 34 tier 3. Bloat | Weak. Armor-only pairs (Light + Restoration) have no identity |
| **Recommended: armor-free hybrids + trait passives (R6, R7)** | Full. Every weapon x magic pair has a class and any armor works in it | 16 tier 3 | Strong. Each class keeps a signature perk and gambits, and armor never defines a hybrid |

### R6. Armor is a free choice inside every hybrid, not a hybrid-defining trait
Armor in Skyrim is a skill tree the player levels freely. In Three Houses, almost no class is defined by armor (only Fortress Knight and Great Knight need a Heavy Armor rank [V5]). So:
- Tier 2 keeps armor as a trait (Knight and Warrior are Heavy, Reaver, Archer and Thief are Light, mages are Cloth). The tier-2 masteries give the ARMOR passives.
- A tier-3 hybrid is defined by its WEAPON x MAGIC pair (or weapon x stealth). It works in any armor, and the follower's mastered armor passives make that armor good.
- This folds all 13 armor pairs (#1 to #13) into existing classes without a class each. Heavy + Destruction (Battlemage) becomes a Spellsword in heavy armor with a shield, Cloth + 2H + Conjuration becomes a Sorcerer in robes, Heavy + Illusion becomes a Nightblade in plate.
- Melee hybrids also leave the weapon form free (1H, 1H + shield, 2H), because the Knight and Warrior masteries already supply 1H, shield and 2H passives.

### Recommended counts per tier
- **Tier 1: 3** (Melee, Ranged, Mage). Unchanged.
- **Tier 2: 9** (Knight, Warrior, Reaver, Archer, Thief, Evoker, Conjurer, Healer, Warlock). Unchanged. They own the trait passives.
- **Tier 3: 16.**
  - 7 pure (eval A's list): Housecarl (Knight), Champion (Warrior + Reaver, merging eval B's Barbarian), Ranger (Archer), Master Thief or Assassin (Thief, name is marth's call), Wizard (Evoker + Warlock), Necromancer (Conjurer), Priest (Healer).
  - 4 melee x school: Spellsword (Destruction, takes in Battlemage), Sorcerer (Conjuration), Templar (Restoration, takes in Crusader and Vigilant), Nightblade (Illusion/Alteration).
  - 4 ranged x school: Witchhunter (Destruction), Warden (Conjuration), Dawnguard (Restoration), Scout (Illusion/Alteration).
  - 1 dual-wield duelist (Light + 1H, Sword-Singer or Rogue), because dual wield is a real Skyrim style that no other class carries.
- **Master: about 6.** One per family: pure melee, pure ranged and stealth, pure mage, melee x magic, ranged x magic, and the stealth killer line. Three Houses has 9 Master classes for a narrower roster [V5], so 6 is lean but enough.
- **Total: 34 classes** (3 + 9 + 16 + 6), close to Three Houses' roster, but with every Skyrim weapon x school pair covered.

### R7. Passives live at the TRAIT level, signatures live at the CLASS level
This is what gives freedom without bloat.
- **Trait passives (shared, about 13 perk lines):** 3 armor, 4 weapon forms (1H, shield, 2H, ranged, with dual wield inside 1H), 4 schools (I/A counted as one), stealth. Each tier-2 class grants the passives of its own traits, permanently once mastered (small rank) and stronger while equipped. Higher tiers do NOT add new copies of these.
- **Class signature (one perk plus 2 or 3 gambits per class, tier 2 and up):** the thing only that class does. For example: Spellsword, a spell hit empowers the next weapon strike. Templar, a ward on block, sun damage on a power attack. Warden, the summon taunts while the follower shoots. Scout, invisibility to break off then a sneak shot. The signature is active ONLY while the class is equipped (R4).
- So a follower's power is "the traits it has mastered" (broad, portable, the Skyrim freedom) plus "the class it wears" (narrow, the FE class feel). Perk count grows with traits plus classes, not with the product of the two.
- This mirrors Fates, where a promoted class grants the skills of every base class that feeds it [V3], and Three Houses, where class abilities stay in the class and mastery abilities travel [V2][V4].

### FE mechanisms that serve all three axes
- **Three Houses mastery abilities kept across classes** [V2]. Freedom (any build keeps what it earned) and no bloat (one ability reused everywhere).
- **Three Houses class abilities and Advanced or Master combat arts locked to their class** [V2][V4]. Strong class feel. This is the model for the equipped-only signature.
- **Three Houses certification by skill rank** [V5]. The closest FE analog to Skyrim's skill-driven builds: the build itself shows what a unit can become. MFO already gates by mastery, but the trait model is the same idea.
- **Awakening and Fates skill carryover** [V4]. Learned power is permanent. Take the carryover, but bound it by small permanent ranks instead of Awakening's 5 slots (R3), since an AI follower should not need slot management.
- **Engage proficiency opened by Emblem bonds** [V9]. A secondary competence opens a cross-line class. Same spirit as letting any one extra mastery open the pure tier 3 (R1).
- **Fates promoted classes granting their feeders' skills** [V3]. Composition instead of duplicate classes, the basis of R7.

### FE mechanisms that work AGAINST Skyrim's freedom (do not copy)
- **Hard weapon locks per class** (an FE mage cannot hold a sword). An MFO class should steer style and gambits, never forbid gear.
- **Fixed promotion lines and 2-way trees** (FE1 to FE7, Sacred Stones, Awakening). An odd mix has no branch to take.
- **Character-decided branches** (Thracia 776 [V1]). Removes player agency.
- **Unit-specific class sets and gender locks** (Awakening, Fates [V3], Three Houses gender-locked classes such as Gremory and War Master [V5]). Every follower should see the same tree.
- **Scarce consumable seals and per-class unit caps** (DS remakes cap units per class family [V3]). Artificial scarcity, not build expression.
- **Level reset on reclass** (Awakening [V3]). A grind loop. MFO has no class level reset, so it is already avoided.
- **Engage class skills lost on reclass** [V4]. Fine for the equipped signature, but bad if applied to mastered passives. Mastered passives must stay permanent.

### Updated recommendation list
KEEP: tier 1 trainee-style trio, 9 tier-2 classes, trait-pair hybrids, pure tier-3 line, free switching, the 3 / 6 / 10 h curve, the permanent-plus-equipped passive shape.
CHANGE: R1 (light second mastery for pure promotions), R2 (XP by participation), R3 (small permanent ranks, no stacking), R6 (armor free inside hybrids, 16 tier 3).
ADD: R4 (equipped-only signature perk and gambits), R5 (optional auto-pick), R7 (trait-level passives, class-level signatures), about 6 Masters.
